System Status

Open Settings > System Status, directly below System Settings, for a read-only overview of your RigPi. This page is available to administrators (access levels 1 and 2).

The readings refresh every 10 seconds while the page is visible. Click Refresh for a new reading. Navigating to or from this page preserves the master shell uptime and audio session.

The Station Health summary highlights readings that need attention and offers practical next steps. Colored bullets appear beside the related readings: green — Normal, yellow — Attention, red — Problem, and gray — Unknown or observing. The words accompany each color. Click a finding to jump to its reading, or expand Show normal checks to review the checks that passed.

Checks cover temperature, fan, power and throttling, CPU use and load, memory, root disk space, and local services. Recommendations are advice only; RigPi does not automatically restart services, reboot, or change settings. A running service does not by itself confirm attached hardware or an external connection.

•Temperature: attention at 75°C sustained for 30 seconds, immediate attention at 80°C, and a problem at 85°C.

•CPU and memory: CPU attention at 85% and problem at 97%; memory attention at 90% and problem at 97%. These conditions must persist for 30 seconds. Load attention begins at one task per core over the last minute.

•Storage: attention at 90% root disk use and a problem at 97%.

•Cooling: a fan reporting zero RPM while cooling is requested or the Pi is hot receives attention after 30 seconds. A stopped fan on a cool Pi can be normal. Missing fan sensors remain unknown.

•Power: current undervoltage is a problem. A historical undervoltage flag receives attention and is clearly identified as a past event. Supply advice follows the Pi model; Pi 5 guidance recommends the official 27W supply or a compatible 5V/5A supply.

•Services: a failed service is a problem immediately. A stopped required service, or an SDR service assigned to an account marked active, receives attention after 20 seconds. Stopped optional services remain unknown and may be intentionally unused. No SDR selected does not imply hardware failure.

Unknown is not a normal result. Missing readings, optional stopped services, and conditions still being observed are listed separately. Observation timing runs while this page receives fresh readings and resets when the page is reopened or readings are interrupted. Failed refreshes turn the summary and reading indicators gray; details then represent the last successful snapshot. The diagnostic summary includes health findings for remote support.

Background health notifications

The master shell checks Station Health approximately every five minutes while an administrator (access level 1 or 2) is logged in. It starts shortly after the shell opens and continues across Tuner, Keyer and other page navigation. A new yellow or red condition is checked again about 30 seconds later before a toast appears. A brief condition that clears is not announced.

 

Each toast includes the condition, a recommendation and a View System Status button. Yellow attention notices disappear after about 20 seconds; red problem notices remain until dismissed or a later reading clears them. Hovering over a yellow notice or using its controls keeps it visible. Dismissed or expired notices do not repeat unless the condition worsens, or is observed to return to normal and then occurs again. Reloading the master shell starts a new monitoring session.

 

Missing readings, intentionally unused optional services and failed health requests do not generate problem toasts. Monitoring pauses when the RigPi browser tab is hidden and resumes when it is visible; browser sleep or suspension can delay checks. This is browser-based monitoring while RigPi is open, not a server alarm service. Notifications offer advice only and never restart services, reboot, transmit or change settings.

Installed version and files

Shows the installed RigPi version, Raspberry Pi model and hardware revision, operating system and kernel. Key file dates identify the last modification of the installed shell, Tuner, Keyer, menu, System Status and SDR files. Local edits change these dates; a file date is not a release number or an online update check.

System health

•Uptime: time since the Raspberry Pi booted.

•CPU use: sampled use of total CPU capacity across all cores. Load averages cover the last 1, 5 and 15 minutes and are shown alongside the core count.

•CPU temperature: the current sensor reading in degrees Celsius.

•Cooling fan: running or stopped, measured speed in RPM, fan drive percentage and cooling level when sensors are available. Fan drive is the control setting, not a percentage of measured maximum RPM. Readings are informational; this page does not change fan control.

•Memory: RAM in use and total usable RAM. The usable total can be slightly below the advertised capacity.

•Swap: swap space in use and total configured swap. A zero used value means swap is available but currently unused.

•Root disk: used and total space on the root filesystem.

Power and throttling

Shows undervoltage, CPU frequency capping, CPU throttling and the soft temperature limit. Each line separates the current condition from events recorded since boot. An earlier event can remain recorded after the condition clears. Normal with None since boot means neither a current condition nor a recorded event was reported.

The firmware reading includes its timestamp. If collection fails or the reading is more than 30 seconds old, the status is marked unavailable. Missing readings are never treated as normal.

Highest CPU consumers

Lists the five processes using the most CPU during a half-second sample, with process name, process ID (PID) and percentage. Percentages use the total capacity of all CPU cores, matching CPU use in System health. Short decoding bursts can change the ranking between refreshes.

Network IP addresses

Lists the RigPi's Ethernet, Wi-Fi and other interface addresses. No IP address means that interface has no assigned address. IPv6 link-local addresses and container addresses are labeled separately.

Connection IP is the address seen by the web server. A local proxy can appear as 127.0.0.1. Reported client IP is supplied in a proxy header and is labeled as reported. It is diagnostic information, not proof of a client's identity.

Current browser

The Network IP addresses section identifies the current browser and reported version, its device type, and whether that device is external to the Raspberry Pi. Mac, Windows, iOS and Android browsers are labeled External device. Linux browsers may run on the Pi or another computer, so they are marked as uncertain when the browser identification cannot distinguish them. These readings describe the browser viewing System Status, not every account. A proxy address of 127.0.0.1 does not mean the browser runs on the Pi.

Connection URL shows the address used by the browser to open RigPi. Connection security identifies HTTPS (encrypted using TLS/SSL) or HTTP (not encrypted). This describes the browser connection, including connections through a remote proxy. The URL omits query parameters and fragments.

Accounts marked active

Shows saved account activity flags, callsigns, available IP information, assigned radios, receiver ports and last visit times. These flags can remain after a browser closes. Last visit is not a live connection heartbeat. Visits older than 24 hours are identified, and missing saved addresses show Not recorded. The current viewer's request can supply an address even when its saved account address is empty.

Radios, receivers and audio

Radios shows configured or assigned radios, available CAT frequency and mode, saved PTT state and serial device detection. A detected USB serial device does not by itself mean the radio is connected. Disconnected or no frequency means no current frequency is available.

Receivers and audio lists receiver engines, selected sources, device state, frequency, sample rate, TX audio and receive loopback. An available engine is separate from the browser's audio play/pause state. No SDR with an idle device can be a normal configuration.

Detected devices lists USB equipment and ALSA audio cards. Services confirms that the web server and database responded to the status request.

Copy diagnostic summary

Click Copy diagnostic summary to copy a formatted HTML snapshot of the displayed readings for troubleshooting. Email and document editors can retain headings, tables and colored health indicators. Plain-text destinations receive a readable text version. The button confirms Copied with formatting (or Copied as text when formatting is unavailable). The summary includes account names, callsigns, IP addresses and device details shown on the page. Review it before sharing.

If a refresh fails, the last successful snapshot remains visible with an explanatory message. Check the reading timestamp before relying on it.

For configuration changes, see System Settings or Audio.

 

Connections and services: Elmer, Cloudflare and DuckDNS show local service or updater state. QRZ, HamQTH and Pushover show saved credential completeness for the signed-in account. Configured does not mean a remote connection has been tested. Elmer pairing is checked in Elmer settings. The overview does not send notifications or change service settings.

 

Keyers shows each configured keying method, serial device availability, and saved speed where available. Rotors shows configured models, ports, and last reported bearings. Saved readings may be stale. Device detection does not verify keying or rotor control. These checks do not transmit or move the antenna.

Access level is read from the current account settings and appears in both Accounts marked active and SDR by account.

 

All accounts are included under SDR by account, including inactive accounts. This overview only displays access levels; it does not change account permissions.

 

Services lists the SDR service instances on ports 8001 through 8005, showing Running, Stopped, Failed or another current service state. Service state is separate from receiver hardware selection and browser audio. A running service can have No SDR selected.

 

Service CPU percentages include worker processes and use total Raspberry Pi CPU capacity: one fully busy core on a four-core Pi is about 25%. Measurements cover approximately half a second at each refresh and can fluctuate. Missing counters show Unavailable. Stopped services show 0.0%. CPU is shown for the web server, PHP, database, SDR services,

 

Cloudflare, Elmer and the DuckDNS updater. The DuckDNS timer schedules updates; its updater service performs the work. Remote services such as QRZ, HamQTH and Pushover have no separate local CPU measurement.

 

Reboot RigPi at the top of System Status restarts the Raspberry Pi after confirmation. It disconnects all users and interrupts radio, SDR and audio services. Wait for the Pi to restart, then open the login page to reconnect. The overview is read-only except for this confirmed administrator action.
